# Data

Three kinds of data, with different rules.

| Folder | What | Committed? | Loader |
|---|---|---|---|
| `data/synthetic/` | Seeded synthetic datasets with known truth | yes (generated, never edited by hand) | `mktstats.data.load_synthetic`, `load_truth`; R reads the CSVs |
| `data/real/` | Small real datasets whose licence allows redistribution | yes | `load_prop99`, `load_sbg_retention` |
| download cache | Larger real datasets, fetched on first use and checked with sha256 | no | `load_cdnow`, `load_mmm_example`, `load_online_retail_ii`, `load_hillstrom` |

The download cache is `$MKTSTATS_CACHE`, default `~/.cache/mktstats`. `MKTSTATS_OFFLINE=1` forbids
downloads; a missing file then raises with the URL to fetch by hand and the path to save it at.
When `mktstats` runs outside a checkout (Colab), committed files are fetched from raw GitHub at
`$MKTSTATS_REF` (default `main`).

## Synthetic data (`data/synthetic/`)

Written by `python scripts/make_synthetic.py` from the generators in `src/mktstats/synth/` with
their default seeds (numpy `Generator(PCG64(seed))`, numpy 2.1.3). `python scripts/make_synthetic.py
--check` exits 1 if regenerating would change any file; CI runs it. All true parameters, the
definitions behind them (ROAS window, units, discounting) and the recovery tolerances the tests use
are in `truth.json`. Generated by this repository's code, under the same licence as the code (MIT).

Retailer time conventions: rates are per week; transactions carry calendar dates; summarize them
with `time_unit="D", time_scaler=7` (pymc-marketing) to get recency and T in weeks. The

### Confounded MMM (Module 9)

`mmm(confounded=True)` keeps the clean MMM's tv, social and display spend, prices and holidays and
adds an unobserved demand shock `d_t` (AR(1), coefficient 0.7, standardized) that multiplies search
spend by `exp(0.15 d_t)` and adds `9,000 d_t` to weekly sales. The strength was chosen by fitting
pymc-marketing 1.2.0 (`mktstats.recovery.fit_mmm`: nutpie, 2 chains, default priors, random seed 1)
on the committed file, 2026-10-09:

| Fit | Draws | Search ROAS mean (truth 3.0) | 94% HDI | Other channels inside |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Uncalibrated | 300 | 5.42 | 4.14 to 6.72 | 3 of 3 |
| Uncalibrated | 1000 | 5.35 | 4.01 to 6.53 | 3 of 3 |
| Both search lift tests | 300 | 3.06 | 2.78 to 3.34 | 3 of 3 |
| Both search lift tests | 1000 | 3.06 | 2.77 to 3.37 | 3 of 3 |
| +30% search test only | 500 | 3.24 | 2.69 to 4.03 | 3 of 3 |
| Switch-off search test only | 500 | 3.51 | 3.13 to 3.81 | 3 of 3 |

Calibration raises the social and display means (to about 2.8 and 1.4 against 1.4 and 0.6) with
wide intervals that still cover the truth: the sales search no longer explains go elsewhere.
Stronger shocks were tried first: with `0.3 d_t` in spend and `15,000 d_t` in sales, even a lift
test with a 5% standard error left calibrated search at 3.47 (3.20 to 3.73), because the 156 weeks
of confounded data outweigh one test.

### Channel vocabulary and long-run value (Modules 10 and 12)

`truth.json` block `channel_value` (from `mktstats.synth.channels`): media channels are where money
is spent, acquisition channels are how a customer first arrived. The link is an assumption of the
synthetic world, not an estimate: tv brings 30% search and 70% referral customers (no click; brand
searches and word of mouth), search 100% search, social 90% social and 10% referral, display 60%
search and 40% social; a new customer costs tv $150, search $150, social $100, display $300 of
media spend, constant within the allocation bounds. A new customer's long-run value is the margin
(30%) on their discounted repeat purchases, without the first purchase, which the MMM's short-run
response already counts. The long-run optimum moves about $5,000 a week from search to tv against
the short-run optimum on Module 9's data (about $8,300 in the capstone), but its value is within
0.5% (capstone 1.1%) of the short-run plan's: check spend by channel, not only value.

### Capstone (Module 12)

`capstone(seed=2032)` is one retailer with its own seeds and parameters: 4,000 customers whose
36-month value at 1% a month is referral $140, search $87, social $53 (margin, first purchase
included); a confounded national MMM (search ROAS 2.6) ending the week before a regional test; a
40-geo panel in which paid search was switched off in 8 geos for 10 weeks (sales lost $85,310 for
$31,200 of spend saved: incremental ROAS 2.73, below the break-even 3.33); the CLV-weighted weekly
plan for a $75,000 budget; and an e-mail offer where targeting on the true CATE is worth $0.51 per
customer against $0.13 for mailing everyone (test split). Each region's response is a scaled copy
of the national curve, so the national-equivalent lift row (x = $18,000, delta_x = -x, delta_y =
r(0) - r(x)) lies exactly on the MMM's true curve. Measured on the committed data (2026-10-09):
synthetic control recovers the sales change within 1.8% with placebo p = 0.03; BG/NBD MAP with
channel covariates recovers the new-customer values within 15% (social +14.8%); the uncalibrated MMM
puts search at 4.06 (2.74 to 5.47, 300 draws) and 3.93 (2.51 to 5.43, 1000 draws), and the lift row
built from the synthetic-control readout brings it to 2.88 (2.51 to 3.19) and 2.86 (2.54 to 3.20),
with all four channels inside; a T-learner rule earns $0.32 and a 200-tree CausalForestDML rule
$0.38 per test customer. A +100% search test in place of the switch-off moved calibrated search
ROAS up (3.92 to 4.71 with a milder shock), because it probes the curve above the observed spend,
not its level; that is why the default test is a switch-off.

The new CSVs are not yet listed in `mktstats.data.SYNTHETIC_TABLES`; until they are, Python labs
call the generators (identical output) and R reads the files with `mkt_data()`.

## Real data committed here (`data/real/`)

### `prop99.csv` — California Proposition 99 (Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller 2010)

- 1,209 rows: 39 states x 1970-2000. Columns: state, year, cigsale (per-capita cigarette sales in
  packs; California 1988 = 90.1), lnincome, beer, age15to24, retprice. Missing values are empty.
- Source: the `smoking` dataset of the R package **tidysynth 0.2.1** (Eric Dunford), CRAN source
  tarball https://cran.r-project.org/src/contrib/tidysynth_0.2.1.tar.gz (sha256
  `046b416b437dd2f3d5610ac6a594db1c941c3cf9c208becfdfed0a76ce553281`), downloaded 2026-10-09.
  tidysynth documents the data as the panel used by Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller (2010),
  "Synthetic control methods for comparative case studies: Estimating the effect of California's
  tobacco control program", JASA 105(490): 493-505, with source https://economics.mit.edu/files/11859.
- Licence: tidysynth is released under MIT (`LICENSE`: YEAR 2020, COPYRIGHT HOLDER Eric Dunford).
- Processing: `data/smoking.rda` loaded in R 4.6.1 (workshop Docker image), written to CSV, sorted
  by state and year, numbers rounded to 7 significant digits (the values are single precision in
  the original; e.g. `89.80000305` becomes `89.8`). File sha256
  `0ba552d2250f8e8bffc6500f33858beff3378a3027213fb7507b019ec6fc3132`.
- Note: tidysynth's help page labels cigsale "pack per 100,000 people"; the values (around 90-130)
  are per-capita packs, the unit used by Abadie et al.

### `sbg_retention.csv` — Fader & Hardie (2007) retention counts

- 13 rows: year 0-12, survivors out of 1,000 customers in the "regular" and "highend" segments.
- Source: Table 1 of P. S. Fader and B. G. S. Hardie (2007), "How to project customer retention",
  Journal of Interactive Marketing 21(1): 76-90
  (https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Fader_hardie_jim_07.pdf).
  The percentages were transcribed from pymc-marketing 1.2.0's notebook
  `docs/source/notebooks/clv/sbg.ipynb` (raw file at tag 1.2.0, fetched 2026-10-09), which states
  that they are Table 1 of the paper and asserts that its simulated cohorts reproduce years 0-7;
  counts = percentage x 10. **Not checked against the PDF itself**: the PDF was downloaded but its
  text could not be extracted (embedded font encoding). The paper's MLE estimates quoted in the
  same notebook: regular alpha = 0.704, beta = 1.182; highend alpha = 0.668, beta = 3.806.
- Licence: survivor counts are published facts from a table, reproduced with citation.
- File sha256 `e619efd9109441a1049f0bc852d47761be383365845154b0f3b5082ef79bdff9`.
